⭕ Circles

Radius

The radius is the distance from the centre of a circle to its edge.

Diameter

The diameter goes through the centre of the circle from one side to the other.

Diameter = 2 × Radius
Radius = Diameter ÷ 2

Circumference

Circumference is the distance around the outside of a circle.

Circumference = 2πr
Circumference = πd

Area of a Circle

Area = πr²
Example:
A circle has radius 7 cm. Using π = 22/7:

Diameter = 2 × 7 = 14 cm
Circumference = 2 × 22/7 × 7 = 44 cm
Area = 22/7 × 7 × 7 = 154 cm²
Common mistake: Do not confuse radius with diameter.

📏 Constructions

Constructing a 90° angle

A perpendicular line creates a 90° angle.

Constructing a 45° angle

First construct a 90° angle, then bisect it.

Angle Bisector

An angle bisector divides an angle into two equal angles.

Perpendicular Bisector

A perpendicular bisector cuts a line segment into two equal parts at 90°.

Equipment

  • Compass
  • Ruler
  • Pencil
  • Protractor when required
Keep your compass point fixed when drawing arcs.

❓ Finding the Unknown

To solve an equation, use inverse operations to isolate the unknown.

Addition ↔ Subtraction
Multiplication ↔ Division
Example:
x + 9 = 17
x = 17 − 9
x = 8
Example:
3x = 21
x = 21 ÷ 3
x = 7

For equations with multiple operations

3x + 5 = 20

3x = 20 − 5
3x = 15
x = 15 ÷ 3
x = 5

📦 Area & Perimeter

Rectangle

Area = Length × Width
Perimeter = 2(Length + Width)

Square

Area = Side × Side
Perimeter = 4 × Side

Triangle

Area = ½ × Base × Height
A rectangle is 12 cm long and 5 cm wide.

Area = 12 × 5 = 60 cm²
Perimeter = 2(12 + 5) = 34 cm
Area is measured in square units such as cm² or m². Perimeter is measured in normal units such as cm or m.

📊 Mean, Median & Range

Mean

Add all the numbers and divide by how many numbers there are.

Mean = Total ÷ Number of values

Median

Put the numbers in order and find the middle value.

Range

Subtract the smallest value from the largest value.

Numbers: 4, 6, 8, 10, 12

Mean = 40 ÷ 5 = 8
Median = 8
Range = 12 − 4 = 8

🧪 Acids, Bases & Neutrals

Acids

Acids have a pH below 7. Some common examples include lemon juice and vinegar.

Neutral substances

Neutral substances have a pH of 7. Pure water is approximately neutral.

Bases / Alkalis

Bases have a pH above 7. Soap solution is an example of an alkaline substance.

pH < 7 → Acidic
pH = 7 → Neutral
pH > 7 → Alkaline

Indicators

Indicators are substances that change colour depending on whether a solution is acidic or alkaline.

Neutralisation

Neutralisation happens when an acid reacts with a base and produces a less acidic or less alkaline mixture.

💡 Light & Reflection

Luminous objects

Luminous objects produce their own light. The Sun and a lit bulb are examples.

Non-luminous objects

Non-luminous objects do not produce their own light. We see them because light reflects from them.

Reflection

Reflection occurs when light bounces off a surface.

Angle of incidence = Angle of reflection

Important terms

  • Incident ray — incoming light ray
  • Reflected ray — outgoing light ray
  • Normal — imaginary line perpendicular to the surface
  • Angle of incidence — angle between incident ray and normal
  • Angle of reflection — angle between reflected ray and normal

🌑 Shadows

A shadow forms when an opaque object blocks light.

Three important things

  1. Light source
  2. Object blocking the light
  3. Surface or screen where the shadow appears

Shadow size

Changing the distance between the light source, object and screen can change the size of the shadow.

Moving an object closer to a light source can make its shadow appear larger on a nearby screen.

Fair tests

In an experiment, change one independent variable while keeping important controlled variables the same.

⚡ Electricity & Circuits

Complete circuit

A complete circuit provides a continuous path for electric current.

Incomplete circuit

An incomplete circuit has a break, so current cannot flow through the entire circuit.

Conductors

Conductors allow electricity to pass through them easily. Most metals are good conductors.

Insulators

Insulators resist the flow of electricity. Plastic and rubber are common examples.

Switches

A closed switch completes a circuit. An open switch breaks the circuit.

Series circuits

Components in a series circuit are connected along the same path.

🫀 Human Digestive System

Mouth / Buccal cavity

Digestion begins in the mouth. Teeth break food into smaller pieces and saliva begins chemical digestion.

Oesophagus

The oesophagus carries food from the mouth to the stomach.

Stomach

The stomach churns food and mixes it with digestive juices.

Small intestine

Most digestion and absorption of nutrients occurs here.

Large intestine

The large intestine absorbs much of the remaining water.

Rectum and anus

The rectum stores waste and the anus is where waste leaves the body.

Remember the pathway:
Mouth → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small intestine → Large intestine → Rectum → Anus

Seed respiration investigation

Germinating seeds release carbon dioxide during respiration. Carbon dioxide can turn limewater milky.

🔬 Experiments & Scientific Thinking

Independent variable

The variable that you deliberately change.

Dependent variable

The variable that you measure or observe.

Controlled variables

Variables kept the same to make the experiment fair.

Hypothesis

A prediction that can be tested through an investigation.

Reliable results

Repeating measurements and looking for consistent results can improve reliability.

🇮🇳 The Constitution of India

What is a Constitution?

A constitution is a set of fundamental rules and principles that explains how a country is governed and how powers and rights are organised.

The Preamble

The Preamble introduces the Constitution and describes important ideals and goals of the Indian Republic.

Fundamental Rights

Fundamental Rights protect important freedoms and equality for citizens.

Fundamental Duties

Fundamental Duties describe responsibilities citizens should follow.

Why is the Constitution important?

  • It establishes the framework of government.
  • It defines powers and responsibilities.
  • It protects important rights.
  • It supports democratic government.
  • It provides principles for governing the country.

🏛️ Legislature, Executive & Judiciary

Legislature

The legislature makes laws.

Executive

The executive carries out and implements laws and government decisions.

Judiciary

The judiciary interprets laws and settles legal disputes.

Legislature → Makes laws
Executive → Implements laws
Judiciary → Interprets laws

🗳️ Democracy

Democracy is a form of government in which people participate in choosing representatives and influencing how they are governed.

Important ideas

  • Equality
  • Participation
  • Representation
  • Rights
  • Responsibilities
  • Rule of law

Important vocabulary

  • Citizen: A legal member of a country.
  • Government: The system that manages public affairs.
  • Law: A rule that must be followed.
  • Rights: Important freedoms and protections.
  • Duty: A responsibility a person is expected to fulfil.

🇦🇪 UAE Values

Respect

Treating other people with consideration and dignity.

Tolerance

Accepting differences between people and communities.

Responsibility

Taking ownership of your actions and duties.

Cooperation

Working together to achieve a shared goal.

Community

People living or working together and supporting one another.

Empathy

Understanding and considering another person's feelings or situation.

Example: Working with classmates and listening respectfully to their ideas demonstrates cooperation and respect.
