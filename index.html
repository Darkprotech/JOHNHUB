⭕ Circles

What is a circle?

A circle is a perfectly round two-dimensional shape in which every point on the boundary is the same distance from the centre. That distance is called the radius.

Important parts

  • Centre: the exact middle of the circle.
  • Radius: distance from the centre to the circumference.
  • Diameter: a line passing through the centre from one side to the other.
  • Circumference: the distance around the outside of the circle.
Diameter = 2 × Radius
Radius = Diameter ÷ 2

Circumference

Circumference means the distance around the circle. When the radius is known, use:

C = 2πr

When the diameter is known, you can use:

C = πd
Example:
A circle has radius 5 cm.
C = 2 × π × 5
C = 10π
Using π ≈ 3.14:
C ≈ 31.4 cm

Area of a circle

Area tells us how much space is inside the circle.

A = πr²
Example:
Radius = 4 cm
A = π × 4²
A = 16π
A ≈ 50.24 cm²
Common mistake: Do not confuse circumference and area. Circumference is measured in units such as cm, while area is measured in square units such as cm².

📐 Constructions

What is geometric construction?

A construction is a precise geometric drawing made using mathematical instruments such as a ruler and compass. Unlike an ordinary sketch, a construction is based on exact relationships and measurements.

Constructing a 90° angle

  1. Draw a straight line.
  2. Choose a point on the line.
  3. Place the compass at that point.
  4. Make equal arcs on either side.
  5. Use the intersection points to construct the perpendicular.

Angle bisector

An angle bisector divides an angle into two equal angles. For example, a 60° angle becomes two 30° angles.

Perpendicular bisector

A perpendicular bisector cuts a line segment into two equal parts while meeting it at 90°.

Useful constructions

Construction Result
Angle bisector Divides an angle into two equal angles
Perpendicular bisector Divides a segment into two equal lengths at 90°
90° angle Right angle
45° angle Half of a 90° angle
60° angle Can be constructed using equal-radius arcs
30° angle Half of a 60° angle

❓ Finding the Unknown

What does an unknown mean?

An unknown is a value that we do not know yet. It is commonly represented by a letter such as x.

Inverse operations

To solve an equation, use the opposite operation to isolate the unknown.

Operation Inverse
AdditionSubtraction
SubtractionAddition
MultiplicationDivision
DivisionMultiplication
Example:
x + 7 = 19
Subtract 7 from both sides.
x = 12
Example:
5x = 35
Divide both sides by 5.
x = 7
Example:
x - 8 = 20
Add 8 to both sides.
x = 28
Always perform the same operation on both sides of an equation. This keeps the equation balanced.

📏 Area & Perimeter

Perimeter

Perimeter is the total distance around the outside of a shape.

Rectangle: P = 2(l + w)
Square: P = 4s

Area

Area measures the amount of space inside a two-dimensional shape.

Rectangle: A = l × w
Square: A = s²
Triangle: A = ½ × base × height
Example:
A rectangle has length 8 cm and width 5 cm.

Area = 8 × 5 = 40 cm²
Perimeter = 2(8 + 5) = 26 cm
Do not use the perimeter formula when the question asks for area. Look carefully at the units and the wording.

📊 Mean, Median & Range

Mean

The mean is found by adding every value and dividing by the number of values.

Mean = Total ÷ Number of values
Numbers: 4, 6, 8, 10
Total = 28
There are 4 values.
Mean = 28 ÷ 4 = 7

Median

The median is the middle value after the numbers have been arranged from smallest to largest.

Numbers: 3, 7, 9, 12, 20
Median = 9

If there are two middle numbers, add those two numbers and divide by 2.

Range

Range = Largest value − Smallest value
Numbers: 4, 8, 11, 15
Range = 15 − 4 = 11

🧪 Acids, Bases & Neutrals

What is an acid?

An acid is a substance that has acidic properties. Acids can occur naturally in foods and in living organisms. Lemon juice contains citric acid, while vinegar contains acetic acid.

What is a base?

A base has properties opposite to many acids. Some bases are found in cleaning products, while others occur naturally.

What is a neutral substance?

A neutral substance is neither acidic nor alkaline. Pure water is approximately neutral at room temperature.

pH scale

The pH scale is commonly shown from 0 to 14. Values below 7 are acidic, 7 is neutral, and values above 7 are alkaline.

pHType
0–6Acidic
7Neutral
8–14Alkaline

Indicators

Indicators are substances that change colour depending on whether a solution is acidic or alkaline. Universal indicator gives different colours across the pH scale.

Neutralisation

When an acid reacts with a base, a neutralisation reaction can occur. The products commonly include a salt and water.

Acid + Base → Salt + Water
Everyday example:
If a substance is too acidic, a suitable alkaline substance can sometimes be used to reduce the acidity. The exact substance used depends on the situation.
Never taste an unknown substance to determine whether it is acidic or alkaline. Scientific testing should use appropriate indicators and safe procedures.

💡 Light & Reflection

What is light?

Light is a form of energy that allows us to see objects. Some objects produce their own light and are called luminous objects. Other objects are visible because light reflects from their surfaces.

Luminous and non-luminous objects

  • Luminous: Sun, stars, lamps and flames.
  • Non-luminous: books, tables, walls and the Moon.

Reflection

Reflection happens when light strikes a surface and changes direction. Smooth surfaces can produce a clear reflection, while rough surfaces scatter light in many directions.

Law of reflection

For a plane mirror, the angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection.

Angle of incidence = Angle of reflection

Regular and diffuse reflection

Regular reflection happens when light reflects from a smooth surface in an orderly way. Diffuse reflection occurs when a rough surface causes the reflected rays to travel in different directions.

🌑 Shadows

How does a shadow form?

A shadow forms when an opaque object blocks some or all of the light travelling from a light source.

A basic shadow experiment needs:

  • A light source
  • An object that blocks light
  • A screen or surface

What changes the size of a shadow?

The size and position of a shadow depend on the positions of the light source, object and screen.

If an object is moved closer to a light source, its shadow can become larger. Moving it farther from the light source can change the size and shape of the shadow.

Fair testing

In an investigation, a fair test changes one main variable while other important variables are kept controlled.

VariableMeaning
Independent variableThe variable deliberately changed
Dependent variableThe variable measured
Control variablesVariables kept the same

⚡ Electricity & Circuits

What is an electric circuit?

An electric circuit is a complete pathway through which electric current can flow. A simple circuit may contain a cell, wires, a switch and a lamp.

Complete and incomplete circuits

When the pathway is complete, current can flow through the circuit. When there is a gap, current cannot flow through the complete pathway.

Conductors

Conductors allow electric current to pass through relatively easily. Many metals are good conductors.

Insulators

Insulators resist the movement of electric charge. Examples include many plastics and rubber materials.

MaterialTypical behaviour
CopperGood conductor
AluminiumGood conductor
PlasticInsulator
RubberInsulator

Series circuits

In a series circuit, components are connected along one main pathway. If the pathway is broken, the complete circuit is interrupted.

Switches

A switch controls whether the circuit is complete or broken. Closing the switch can complete the pathway; opening it creates a gap.

🫀 Human Digestive System

Why do we digest food?

Food contains substances that our bodies need for energy, growth and repair. Digestion breaks large food molecules into smaller substances that can be absorbed and used by the body.

Main organs

OrganMain role
Mouth / Buccal cavityFood is chewed and mixed with saliva
OesophagusMoves food toward the stomach
StomachChurns food and mixes it with digestive juices
Small intestineDigestion continues and nutrients are absorbed
Large intestineAbsorbs much of the remaining water
RectumStores faeces before removal
AnusOpening through which faeces leave the body

Buccal cavity

The buccal cavity is the mouth region. Teeth mechanically break food into smaller pieces while the tongue helps move and mix the food. Saliva begins the digestion of certain food components.

Stomach

The stomach is a muscular organ. It churns food and mixes it with digestive juices, producing a semi-liquid mixture.

Small intestine

The small intestine is extremely important because digested nutrients are absorbed through its walls into the bloodstream.

Seed respiration investigation

Germinating seeds respire. During respiration, seeds use oxygen and release carbon dioxide. Carbon dioxide can be tested using limewater. When carbon dioxide is passed into limewater, the limewater becomes milky.

Seed respiration → Carbon dioxide → Limewater turns milky

🧪 Experiments & Scientific Thinking

A good scientific investigation

  1. Ask a clear question.
  2. Make a prediction or hypothesis.
  3. Identify variables.
  4. Plan a fair test.
  5. Collect observations or measurements.
  6. Record results clearly.
  7. Look for patterns.
  8. Draw a conclusion based on evidence.
  9. Suggest improvements or further questions.

Why repeat experiments?

Repeating measurements can help identify unusual results and improve confidence in the pattern observed. Scientists should not simply ignore a result because it is unexpected; they should investigate it.

Tables and graphs

Tables are useful for recording exact values. Graphs can make patterns easier to see. The type of graph should match the data being collected.

🇮🇳 The Constitution of India

What is a constitution?

A constitution is a fundamental set of rules and principles that explains how a country is governed. It establishes important institutions, describes powers and responsibilities, and sets out important principles that guide the relationship between citizens and the state.

Why does India have a Constitution?

India is a large and diverse democratic country. A written Constitution provides a framework for governing the country while protecting important principles and rights. It also explains how the different organs of government function.

The Preamble

The Preamble introduces the Constitution and describes important ideals of the Indian Republic. It identifies India as a sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic and expresses goals including justice, liberty, equality and fraternity.

Fundamental Rights

Fundamental Rights are important constitutional protections. They help protect individual liberty and equality and place limits on the exercise of state power.

Fundamental Duties

Fundamental Duties describe responsibilities expected from citizens. They include respecting the Constitution and national symbols, promoting harmony, protecting the environment and developing a scientific temper.

Three organs of government

Organ Main role
Legislature Makes laws
Executive Implements laws and manages administration
Judiciary Interprets laws and resolves legal disputes

Democracy

Democracy is a system in which people participate in choosing their representatives and in public decision-making. Elections are one important way citizens participate in a democracy.

Remember: The Constitution is not simply a list of ordinary laws. It provides the fundamental framework within which government operates.

Important vocabulary

  • Constitution: fundamental framework of government.
  • Democracy: government based on participation of the people.
  • Republic: a state whose head of state is not a hereditary monarch.
  • Equality: equal status and treatment under the law.
  • Justice: fairness in society and law.
  • Liberty: freedom within the framework of law.
  • Fraternity: sense of brotherhood and unity.

🇦🇪 UAE Values

What are values?

Values are principles that help people decide how they should behave toward themselves, other people and their community. Values influence decisions, relationships and responsible citizenship.

Respect

Respect means treating other people, their ideas, cultures and rights with consideration. A respectful community allows people to communicate even when they disagree.

Tolerance

Tolerance means accepting that people can have different cultures, backgrounds, traditions and viewpoints. It encourages peaceful coexistence.

Responsibility

Responsible citizens understand that their choices can affect other people and the environment. Responsibility includes following rules, looking after shared spaces and contributing positively to society.

Cooperation

Cooperation means working together toward a shared goal. Schools, communities and countries often need cooperation to solve problems.

Community

A strong community depends on people helping one another, respecting shared rules and taking care of public spaces.
