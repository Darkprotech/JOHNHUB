➗ Maths Hub

1. Circles

Radius: Distance from the centre to the edge.

Diameter: Distance across the circle through the centre.

Diameter = 2 × radius

Circumference: Distance around a circle.

Circumference = 2πr

Area: Space inside the circle.

Area = πr²

Use π = 22/7 when the question tells you to.

2. Constructions

Important equipment

  • Compass
  • Ruler
  • Protractor
  • Pencil

Common constructions

  • 90° angle
  • 45° angle
  • Angle bisector
  • Perpendicular bisector

An angle bisector divides an angle into two equal angles.

3. Finding the Unknown

Use inverse operations.

3x + 5 = 20

3x = 15
x = 5

Addition ↔ subtraction

Multiplication ↔ division

4. Area and Perimeter

Rectangle:

Area = length × width
Perimeter = 2(length + width)

Square:

Area = side × side
Perimeter = 4 × side

Triangle:

Area = ½ × base × height

5. Mean, Median and Range

Mean: Add all values and divide by the number of values.

Median: Middle value when numbers are arranged in order.

Range: Largest value − smallest value.

Numbers: 4, 7, 8, 10, 11
Mean = 40 ÷ 5 = 8
Median = 8
Range = 11 − 4 = 7

🔬 Science Hub

1. Acids, Bases and Neutrals

The pH scale goes from 0 to 14.

  • pH below 7 → acidic
  • pH 7 → neutral
  • pH above 7 → alkaline/basic
Lemon juice → acidic
Pure water → neutral
Soap solution → alkaline

Indicators

Indicators change colour depending on whether a substance is acidic or alkaline.

Neutralisation: An acid and a base react to form products that are closer to neutral.

2. Light and Reflection

Luminous objects produce their own light.

Non-luminous objects do not produce their own light.

Reflection

Reflection happens when light bounces off a surface.

Angle of incidence = Angle of reflection

Incident ray: Incoming light ray.

Reflected ray: Light ray that bounces away.

Normal: Imaginary line perpendicular to the surface.

3. Shadows

A shadow forms when an opaque object blocks light.

Shadow size can change depending on the distance between:

  • Light source
  • Object
  • Screen

For a fair test, change only one variable at a time.

4. Electricity and Circuits

A complete circuit allows electric current to flow.

An incomplete circuit has a break and current cannot flow.

Conductors

Materials that allow electricity to pass through easily.

Insulators

Materials that resist the flow of electricity.

Examples of conductors include copper and aluminium.

Examples of insulators include plastic and rubber.

5. Human Digestive System

  1. Mouth / buccal cavity
  2. Oesophagus
  3. Stomach
  4. Small intestine
  5. Large intestine
  6. Rectum
  7. Anus

Digestion breaks large food molecules into smaller molecules that the body can absorb and use.

6. Scientific Thinking

A good experiment includes:

  • A question
  • A prediction
  • Variables
  • A method
  • Results
  • A conclusion

In a fair test, keep control variables the same.

Fundamental Duties

Three important organs

2. Reading Paragraph — فقرة قراءة

أنا أحب الملابس الجميلة والمريحة. في فصل الصيف أرتدي قميصاً خفيفاً وبنطالاً قصيراً وحذاءً مريحاً. أما في فصل الشتاء فأرتدي معطفاً دافئاً ووشاحاً وقبعة. أحب اللون الأزرق لأنه لون جميل.

Meaning

I like beautiful and comfortable clothes. In summer, I wear a light shirt, short trousers and comfortable shoes. In winter, I wear a warm coat, a scarf and a hat. I like blue because it is a beautiful colour.

Reading strategy

  1. Read the title.
  2. Underline words you recognise.
  3. Look for names, colours and clothing words.
  4. Use the sentence around an unfamiliar word to understand it.
  5. Answer using information directly from the paragraph.

🇫🇷 French Hub

1. Production écrite

Production écrite means written production.

When writing a paragraph in French, focus on:

  • Clear sentences
  • Correct verb forms
  • Correct gender and agreement
  • Connectors
  • Good vocabulary
  • Correct punctuation

Useful connectors

et — and
mais — but
parce que — because
aussi — also
d'abord — first
ensuite — next
enfin — finally
J'aime porter un jean et un t-shirt parce que c'est confortable. Le week-end, je porte aussi des baskets.

2. L'impératif

The imperative is used to give commands, instructions or advice.

Examples

Écoute ! — Listen!
Regarde ! — Look!
Mange ! — Eat!
Finis ! — Finish!
Prenez ! — Take!

Important

With the imperative, we normally use:

tu → command to one person

nous → let's...

vous → command to several people or polite command

Parle ! — Speak!
Parlons ! — Let's speak!
Parlez ! — Speak!

Negative imperative

Ne parle pas ! — Don't speak!
Ne mange pas ! — Don't eat!

3. Les verbes pronominaux — Reflexive Verbs

Reflexive verbs describe actions done to oneself.

Je me lève. — I get up.
Tu te lèves. — You get up.
Il se lève. — He gets up.
Elle se lève. — She gets up.
Nous nous levons. — We get up.
Vous vous levez. — You get up.
Ils se lèvent. — They get up.

Common reflexive verbs

  • se lever — to get up
  • se laver — to wash oneself
  • se brosser — to brush
  • s'habiller — to get dressed
  • se coucher — to go to bed
  • se réveiller — to wake up

Negative form

Je ne me lève pas tôt.
I don't get up early.

📖 English Hub

1. Tenses

Present Simple

Used for habits, facts and repeated actions.

I play football every Saturday.

Present Continuous

Used for something happening now.

I am studying English.

Past Simple

Used for completed actions in the past.

I visited London last year.

Past Continuous

I was reading when the phone rang.

Future

I will visit Paris next year.

Present Perfect

I have finished my homework.

2. Dialogue Writing

A dialogue is a conversation between two or more characters.

Remember:

  • Give each speaker a new line.
  • Use quotation marks when required.
  • Use speech punctuation correctly.
  • Make the conversation sound natural.
  • Show the characters' personalities.
"Where are you going?" asked Sara.
"I'm going to the library," replied Adam.
"Can I come with you?"
"Of course!"

3. Travelogue Writing

A travelogue is a piece of writing describing a journey or travel experience.

Include:

  • Where you went
  • When you went
  • What you saw
  • What you did
  • What you ate
  • How you felt
  • Interesting details

Useful structure

Introduction → Journey → Places → Experiences → Feelings → Conclusion

Use descriptive language so the reader can imagine being there.

6. P.A.W.S. Paragraph Technique

Use P.A.W.S. to structure a strong literature paragraph.

P — Point
Make your main argument.

A — Answer / Analysis
Explain what the writer is showing.

W — Writer's Method
Identify a technique or word choice.

S — Significance
Explain why it matters and its effect on the reader.

Example — The Eagle

Point: Tennyson presents the eagle as powerful and dominant.

Analysis: The eagle is shown as being high above everything else.

Writer's Method: Tennyson uses powerful imagery and personification.

Significance: This makes the eagle seem majestic and superior to the world below it.

7. P.A.W.S. — The Tyger

Point: Blake presents the tiger as frightening but beautiful.

Analysis: The speaker is fascinated by the creature's powerful appearance.

Writer's Method: Blake uses imagery and rhetorical questions.

Significance: This makes the reader think about the mystery and power behind the tiger's creation.

8. V.O.I.C.E. Framework

Use V.O.I.C.E. to analyse texts such as Whale of the Wild and Free Willy.

V — Vocabulary
Look at important words and phrases.

O — Organisation
Look at how the text is structured.

I — Imagery
Look for descriptions and pictures created by language.

C — Character / Context
Think about the characters, situation and context.

E — Effect
Explain the effect on the reader.

9. Whale of the Wild

Things to look for

  • Human impact on nature

  • Evidence from the text

Use V.O.I.C.E. to organise your analysis.
