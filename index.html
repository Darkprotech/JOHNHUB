➗ Maths Hub

1. Circles

Radius: Distance from the centre to the edge.

Diameter: Distance across the circle through the centre.

Diameter = 2 × radius

Circumference = 2πr

Area = πr²

Remember that the diameter is always twice the radius.

2. Constructions

Important equipment:

  • Compass
  • Ruler
  • Protractor
  • Pencil

Common constructions include:

  • 90° angles
  • 45° angles
  • Angle bisectors
  • Perpendicular bisectors

An angle bisector divides an angle into two equal angles.

3. Finding the Unknown

3x + 5 = 20

3x = 15
x = 5

Use inverse operations:

Addition ↔ subtraction

Multiplication ↔ division

4. Area and Perimeter

Rectangle:
Area = length × width
Perimeter = 2(length + width)

Square:
Area = side × side
Perimeter = 4 × side

Triangle:
Area = ½ × base × height

5. Mean, Median and Range

Mean: Total ÷ number of values.

Median: Middle value.

Range: Highest − lowest.

4, 7, 8, 10, 11
Mean = 40 ÷ 5 = 8
Median = 8
Range = 11 − 4 = 7

🔬 Science Hub

1. Acids, Bases and Neutrals

The pH scale goes from 0 to 14.

  • Below 7 = acidic
  • 7 = neutral
  • Above 7 = alkaline

Indicators change colour depending on the substance.

Lemon juice → acidic
Water → neutral
Soap solution → alkaline

2. Light and Reflection

Luminous objects produce their own light.

Non-luminous objects do not produce their own light.

Angle of incidence = Angle of reflection

The normal is an imaginary line perpendicular to the surface.

3. Shadows

A shadow forms when an opaque object blocks light.

Shadow size can change depending on the positions of:

  • Light source
  • Object
  • Screen

A fair test changes only one variable at a time.

4. Electricity and Circuits

A complete circuit allows current to flow.

An incomplete circuit has a break.

Conductors: allow electricity to flow.

Insulators: resist the flow of electricity.

Copper → conductor
Plastic → insulator

5. Human Digestive System

  1. Mouth / buccal cavity
  2. Oesophagus
  3. Stomach
  4. Small intestine
  5. Large intestine
  6. Rectum
  7. Anus

Digestion breaks food into smaller substances that can be absorbed.

6. Scientific Thinking

A good investigation includes:

  • Question
  • Prediction
  • Variables
  • Method
  • Results
  • Conclusion

1. Constitution of India

A constitution contains fundamental rules and principles for governing a country.

The Preamble

The Preamble expresses important ideals such as:

Justice Liberty Equality Fraternity

Fundamental Rights

Fundamental Rights protect important freedoms and equality.

Fundamental Duties

Citizens have responsibilities towards the country and society.

2. Democracy

Democracy allows people to participate in choosing their representatives.

  • Legislature: Makes laws.
  • Executive: Implements laws.
  • Judiciary: Interprets laws and provides justice.

3. UAE Values

  • Respect
  • Tolerance
  • Responsibility
  • Cooperation
  • Empathy
  • Community

These values help people live and work together peacefully.

🇫🇷 French Hub

1. Production écrite

Production écrite means written production.

Remember:

  • Clear sentences
  • Correct verb forms
  • Correct gender
  • Correct agreements
  • Useful connectors
  • Good vocabulary
et — and
mais — but
parce que — because
aussi — also
d'abord — first
ensuite — next
enfin — finally

2. L'impératif

The imperative is used for commands, instructions and advice.

Écoute ! — Listen!
Regarde ! — Look!
Mange ! — Eat!
Parle ! — Speak!
Prenez ! — Take!

The main forms are:

tu → one person

nous → let's...

vous → several people / polite command

Parle !
Parlons !
Parlez !

Negative:

Ne parle pas ! — Don't speak!

3. Les verbes pronominaux

Reflexive verbs describe actions done to oneself.

Je me lève.
Tu te lèves.
Il se lève.
Elle se lève.
Nous nous levons.
Vous vous levez.
Ils se lèvent.

Common verbs

  • se lever — to get up
  • se laver — to wash
  • se brosser — to brush
  • s'habiller — to get dressed
  • se coucher — to go to bed
  • se réveiller — to wake up

📖 English Hub

1. Tenses

Present Simple

I play football every Saturday.

Present Continuous

I am studying English.

Past Simple

I visited London last year.

Past Continuous

I was reading when the phone rang.

Future

I will visit Paris next year.

Present Perfect

I have finished my homework.

2. Dialogue Writing

  • Give each speaker a new line.
  • Use correct punctuation.
  • Make the conversation natural.
  • Show personality.
  • Use speech verbs correctly.
"Where are you going?" asked Sara.
"I'm going to the library," replied Adam.
"Can I come with you?"
"Of course!"

3. Travelogue Writing

A travelogue describes a journey or travel experience.

Include:

  • Where you went
  • When you went
  • What you saw
  • What you did
  • What you ate
  • How you felt
Introduction → Journey → Places → Experiences → Feelings → Conclusion

4. The Tyger — William Blake

The poem explores the mysterious and powerful nature of the tiger and questions how such a frightening and beautiful creature could have been created.

Themes

  • Creation
  • Power
  • Good and evil
  • Beauty
  • Fear
  • Mystery

Techniques

  • Rhetorical questions
  • Repetition
  • Imagery
  • Symbolism
  • Alliteration

5. The Eagle — Alfred, Lord Tennyson

The poem presents the eagle as powerful, isolated and majestic.

Themes

  • Power
  • Nature
  • Freedom
  • Strength
  • Isolation

Techniques

  • Personification
  • Simile
  • Imagery
  • Alliteration
  • Metaphor

6. P.A.W.S.

P — Point
Make your main argument.

A — Analysis
Explain what the writer is showing.

W — Writer's Method
Identify the technique or important word.

S — Significance
Explain the effect and why it matters.

Use this structure for paragraphs about The Eagle and The Tyger.

7. V.O.I.C.E.

V — Vocabulary
Important words and phrases.

O — Organisation
How the text is structured.

I — Imagery
Descriptions and pictures created by language.

C — Character / Context
Characters, situation and context.

E — Effect
The effect on the reader.

Use this framework when studying Whale of the Wild and Free Willy.
